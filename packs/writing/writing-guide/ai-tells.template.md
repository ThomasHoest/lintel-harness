# AI tells, the screen

A final pass on any Thomas-facing draft (LinkedIn posts, the pitch deck, cover letters, outreach, mail replies) to remove the giveaways that make writing read as AI-generated, while keeping his voice. Apply it while drafting too, so the tells don't go in to begin with.

Target: natural human writing in his register (see [tone-of-voice.md](tone-of-voice.md)). Not tell-avoidance so aggressive that it becomes its own artifact. Judgement over checklist: it's the accumulation of tells that reads as machine-made, not one stray mark.

## The tells to screen for

### Punctuation and typography
- **Em-dashes. The signature tell.** Replace with a comma, full stop, or parentheses. Hard rule for LinkedIn in English. In Danish the tankestreg is legitimate punctuation, so it's less of a giveaway; still prefer commas and periods, and flag heavy use.
- Curly-quote flourishes where plain marks fit.
- Over-using the colon-then-list construction.

### Structural cadence
- **Rule of three.** "faster, cleaner, and more reliable." Real writing is lumpier. Break the triad or cut one. (Three or four item bullet *lists* are fine; it's the rhetorical prose triad that reads as AI.)
- **"It's not just X, it's Y"** and its cousins ("not only… but…", "more than just…"). Delete the frame, state the point.
- **Over-signposting.** "Importantly,", "Ultimately,", "At its core,", "The key takeaway is". Cut them; let the sentence carry itself.
- Uniform rhythm (every sentence and paragraph the same shape). Vary it.

### Diction
- Corporate-abstract nouns: leverage, delve, landscape, realm, tapestry, journey (as cliché), robust, seamless, unlock, elevate, foster, navigate, testament, resonate.
- Empty intensifiers: truly, incredibly, deeply, remarkably, game-changing, cutting-edge.
- Hedge-stacking: "It's worth noting that it could potentially, in some cases…". Say it or don't.
- The summarising outro: "In conclusion,", "At the end of the day," a neat bow that restates what was already said.

### Tone
- Relentless positivity with no friction. His voice has edges and admits difficulty.
- Motivational-poster register. Hype where a plain statement would do.
- Generic where a specific is available. A real detail (a product, a year, a name) beats a smooth abstraction.

## Procedure

1. Read once for **voice**: does it sound like Thomas? Flag what doesn't.
2. **Punctuation** pass: hunt em-dashes first (the one non-negotiable for LinkedIn in English).
3. **Structural cadence** pass: rule of three, "not just X but Y", signposting, uniform rhythm.
4. **Diction** pass: the word and phrase list above.
5. **Tone** pass: hype, frictionlessness, generic-where-specific.
6. Rewrite flagged spans in his register. **Prefer cutting over rephrasing.**
7. Read aloud mentally: if a person wouldn't say it in conversation, it stays flagged.
8. Note any substantive change so Thomas can veto.

## Boundaries

- Screen, don't ghost-write a new voice. Preserve his meaning and register.
- The assistant edits; **Thomas sends and posts.**
- These are heuristics, not laws.
