# `background/strategy/` — where the organisation says it is going

Raw material about direction: the vision as stated, the strategy
documents behind it, the goals someone is measured against, and — the
part that earns this folder its place — **the prior roadmaps and what
actually became of them.**

## What belongs here

- **The vision statement**, in whatever form it exists: a slide, a
  paragraph on a wall, a founder's email, a board narrative.
- **Strategy documents** — a strategy deck, a three-year plan, a
  board-approved plan, a strategy memo, a positioning document.
- **Goals and OKRs** — the current set, and the previous set. Both.
- **Prior roadmaps, with their outcomes.** Last year's roadmap next to
  what shipped. A quarterly plan next to what happened in the quarter.
  **A roadmap without its outcome is half a document**; the delta is the
  evidence.
- **Decisions already made elsewhere** — a board minute, a founder's
  call, an investor condition. Decisions the portfolio does not own but
  must plan around.
- **Superseded strategy.** Keep it, mark it superseded, and date the
  supersession. An abandoned strategy is evidence about how this
  organisation makes and unmakes decisions.

## What the pack does with it

| Material | Where it lands |
|---|---|
| Vision, and the period it covers | **§3 Vision** — including "what has to become true", which is a list of conditions and therefore a list of candidate bets feeding intake directly |
| Strategy documents, goals, OKRs | **§4 Purpose of the portfolio work** — what decisions this portfolio exists to make |
| Decisions made by a board or a founder | **§4**'s second line — *decisions made elsewhere, and by whom*. Saying so plainly is what keeps the register from becoming fiction |
| Prior roadmaps and their outcomes | **§5 Current portfolio**'s honest-status column, and the first `learn` pass. A roadmap that did not happen tells you what this organisation's real horizon was |
| Goal-setting cadence | Cross-check on **§8**'s calendar backstop — cadence follows `U`, not the quarter |

§3 asks for the vision's period *in the units §2 implies*. That
translation is only possible if this folder and `constraints/` are both
filled: a three-year vision in a business with a nine-month certification
cycle contains about four decisions, and the same three years in a fast
market contain twelve.

## Raw is fine

**Paste the deck in.** Whole, with its own headings and its own
overclaiming. **Do not summarise prematurely** — a strategy document's
hedges, its omissions and the goal that quietly disappeared between two
versions are evidence, and they are the first thing a summary removes.

Where two documents disagree, keep both and say they disagree. That
contradiction is a §11 open question, not an error to tidy away.

## Provenance

```
**Source:** <who or what this came from>
**Date of the information:** <YYYY-MM-DD — when it was true, not when you saved it>
**Collected by:** <the user, or the agent that gathered it>
**Class:** <frame | mechanism | vendor magnitude>
```

Most of this folder is **frame** — a way of seeing the business. Label it
so. **A frame may not be cited as evidence of a magnitude:** a strategy
deck asserting a market will triple is a frame, and it does not become a
number because it was printed next to one.

**Undated material is treated as unreliable**, and this folder is where
that bites hardest — an undated strategy document reads as the current
strategy forever. **A §3 or §4 claim traceable to nothing here is a claim
to re-check.** Mark it `[NEEDS SOURCE]`. Never fabricate a citation.

## Who writes here

The **user**, who has the documents. `learning-synthesiser` is the
plausible agent writer: at the `learn` phase it is the role that records
what became of a prior roadmap, which is exactly this folder's most
valuable content. `discovery-lead` writes here when direction has to be
gathered rather than supplied. `gate-reviewer` and `target-reviewer`
never write here. Provisional, as the roles are.
