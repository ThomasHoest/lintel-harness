## 8. Screening for AI tells

A final pass (and a while-drafting habit) that removes the giveaways which make
writing read as AI-generated, while keeping the voice above. **Judgement over
checklist: it's the accumulation of tells that reads as machine-made, not one
stray mark.** The structural and tone tells are largely language-independent;
the punctuation/diction lists are language-specific — **write a native list per
non-English locale, don't translate the English one** (each language's machine
writing has its own stock words, plus anglicisms from hasty translation).

**Structural cadence:** rule-of-three in prose; the "not just X, it's Y" frame
(and "not only… but…", "more than just…"); over-signposting ("Importantly,",
"Ultimately,", "At its core,"); uniform rhythm; the summarising outro ("In
conclusion,", "At the end of the day,"). Break, cut, or vary.

**Tone:** relentless positivity with no friction; motivational-poster hype;
generic where a specific (a product, year, name) is available; hedge-stacking.

**Punctuation & diction — English:** em-dashes (the signature tell — comma /
full stop / parentheses instead; hard rule for LinkedIn/long-form); curly-quote
flourishes; corporate-abstract nouns (*leverage, delve, landscape, realm,
tapestry, journey, robust, seamless, unlock, elevate, foster, navigate*); empty
intensifiers (*truly, incredibly, deeply, game-changing, cutting-edge*).

**Punctuation & diction — `<locale>`:** `<native stock words + anglicisms + the
punctuation notes for this language; mark ⚠ draft until a native speaker signs
off>`.
